Clear-Span, Structural or Rack-Supported

The structure is chosen around what has to happen underneath the floor.

Clear-span structural mezzanines

A structural mezzanine stands on its own steel columns, independent of the racking or equipment beneath it. Wider column spacing keeps forklift aisles and production lines open.

Our modular system spans up to 6 metres between columns. Custom Hi-Span structures go further: the VS Ballet floor spans 9.8 metres clear over a completely unobstructed ground floor.

Rack-supported platforms

Where the storage layout is settled and height is tight, the deck can sit on the racking below. The racking is assessed to AS 4084 as part of the design.

The platform, racking and access are designed, engineered and installed as a single project rather than supplied as separate products.

Modular or custom

Our modular mezzanine system uses standard beam lengths from 3200 to 4800 mm and column heights from 2580 to 3400 mm, which keeps design and installation fast.

Custom mezzanine floors are built for unusual shapes, clearances and loads of up to 1000 kg/m².

Is Your Warehouse Suitable for a Mezzanine Floor?

Four things decide whether a mezzanine will work in your building. A free site consultation confirms all four before design work starts.

Clear height

You need 2,100 mm of headroom above and below the floor. With a minimum floor depth of 250 mm, the practical starting point is a clear roof height of 4,450 mm.

Load rating

Modular floors are rated to 3 kPa (300 kg/m²) for offices and light storage, or 5 kPa (500 kg/m²) for heavy storage. Custom builds go up to 1000 kg/m².

Slab and columns

No new footings are usually needed, but the existing slab carries the column loads, so it is checked on site. Column positions are set around forklift routes, racking and doors.

Access and standards

Stairs, landings and handrails are built to AS 1657. Stair positions, pallet gates and forklift approach are fixed at the same time as the footprint.

Not sure what your floor can take? Read our guide to understanding load ratings for mezzanine floors, or book a free site consultation and we will measure it with you.

Mezzanine Permits and Compliance in Victoria

Most mezzanine floors in Victoria need a building permit before work starts on site. We prepare the documentation and manage the approval through to final sign-off.

We manage the permit for you

Permit requirements depend on the floor area, how the space will be used and the site itself. We confirm them at the site consultation, then handle every approval so your project stays on track.

  • Site check, including whether town planning is involved (for example car parking or a heritage overlay)
  • Drawings and engineering certification to the National Construction Code, AS 1170, AS 4100 and AS 1657
  • Building permit issued by a registered building surveyor before installation
  • Inspection and final sign-off once the mezzanine is installed

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a mezzanine floor cost in Melbourne?

Cost depends on the floor area, load rating, column spacing, decking, stairs, gates and handrails, and the permits your site needs. We prepare a detailed quote once we understand your space, starting from photos or sketches and confirmed at a free site consultation.

What are the regulations for mezzanine floors in Australia?

Mezzanine floors must comply with the National Construction Code and the relevant Australian Standards, including AS 1170 for structural loads, AS 4100 for steel structures and AS 1657 for stairs, landings and handrails. Building codes also require at least 2100 mm of headroom above and below the floor. Every Dynamic mezzanine is supplied with full engineers certification of design.

Do I need a building permit for a mezzanine floor in Melbourne?

In most cases, yes. A mezzanine floor in Victoria generally needs a building permit issued by a registered building surveyor before work starts on site. Dynamic prepares the drawings and engineering, lodges the documentation and manages the approval through to final sign-off.

Do I need planning permission to install a mezzanine floor?

Usually not for an internal mezzanine, but some sites do need town planning approval, for example where the added floor area changes car parking requirements or the property sits under a heritage overlay. We check this with your council before the design is finalised.

What roof height do I need for a mezzanine floor?

You need a minimum headroom clearance of 2100 mm both below and above the mezzanine floor. Since the floor structure takes up a minimum of 250 mm of vertical space, you will generally need a clear roof height of 4450 mm or more.

What load rating does my mezzanine need?

Our modular system is rated to 3 kPa (about 300 kg/m²) for offices and light storage, or 5 kPa (about 500 kg/m²) for heavy storage. Custom mezzanines can be engineered to higher loads, up to 1000 kg/m². The right rating depends on what the floor will carry and is confirmed at the site consultation.

How long does mezzanine installation take?

Installation is fast because the components are pre-engineered and prefabricated. Our crews typically install 50 to 100 square metres per day, so most mezzanine floors are installed within a few days to a couple of weeks, depending on size and complexity.

Do mezzanine floors need footings or foundations?

No. Our prefabricated mezzanine designs typically do not need additional foundations. The existing slab is checked at the site consultation to confirm it can carry the column loads.

Can I expand or relocate my mezzanine in the future?

Yes. Our modular mezzanine floors are designed for flexibility, so they can be extended or relocated as your business changes.

Which areas of Melbourne do you service?

We design and install mezzanine floors across metropolitan Melbourne and regional Victoria, including the industrial areas of Derrimut, Truganina, Laverton North, Campbellfield, Tullamarine, Bayswater and Dandenong South.
